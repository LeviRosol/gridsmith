A few months ago I plugged the old Ender 3 back in and started digging through boxes of terrain tiles I had printed over the years. I had an active D&D campaign going, and after years of Roll20 / DnDBeyond / Google Meet sessions since Covid, I wanted to bring more focus back to the physical table again.

At the same time, I had just finished printing a bunch of Gridfinity storage stuff for my house, and something clicked in my brain:

> What if terrain tiles snapped into a reusable grid instead of relying on magnets or clips?

That idea turned into GridSmith.

At first, I designed a thin reusable grid that worked with some of my existing OpenForge tiles. Honestly, it worked surprisingly well. The grid sat hidden underneath the tiles, kept everything aligned, and eliminated the need for magnets entirely.

But then I started thinking harder about the tiles themselves.

Why are walls consuming so much playable space? Why are bases so thick? Why does storage always become a giant pile of random pieces? Why does a simple plate of tiles turn into a 24-hour print job?

And honestly, I realized a lot of people probably never fully get into printed terrain because of that friction.

So I started designing around a different goal:
- faster prints
- easier storage
- beginner-friendly workflows
- modular layouts
- no magnets
- no clips
- no complicated setup

GridSmith started on a completely stock Ender 3 because I wanted to make sure it worked well for the kind of printer a lot of people actually own. Since then I’ve upgraded to a K2 and started optimizing around what modern printers are capable of.

I also wanted the system to be approachable from a cost standpoint.

All low-resolution GridSmith tiles are completely free on the maker platforms:

- [Printables](https://www.printables.com/@Gridsmithio_4636544)
- [MakerWorld](https://makerworld.com/en/@iveld)
- [Thingiverse](https://www.thingiverse.com/Gridsmithio)

The GridSmith tile builder itself is also free to use.

The only paid portion of the project is the higher-resolution STL files that help fund continued development.

Right now the Tavern Core Set is the primary release, but the current roadmap is roughly:

- Tavern
- Dungeon
- Cave
- Outdoor / Wilderness

Dungeon tiles are probably next, and honestly will likely become the “starter” set for the system.

Anyways, I will be writing more in the coming weeks about the process I went through. The various iterations. The failures.

No, GridSmith is not intended to integrate with every existing terrain ecosystem. It’s really meant for someone new to printable terrain - or someone willing to move to a simpler system.

But I’d genuinely love feedback either way.

Thanks!
