## There are already a lot of incredible terrain systems out there.

Seriously.

GridSmith exists because I spent years printing and using many of them, and along the way I kept running into the same problems over and over again. Not bad design problems - most of these systems are extremely well done - but friction problems.

Terrain printing can become a grind. Huge print times. Magnets. Clips. Assembly. Endless piles of pieces. Storage bins full of random parts. Systems that technically work great, but slowly become hard to manage in real life.

And honestly, I think a lot of people never fully get into printable terrain because of that friction.

At the same time, modern printers have gotten *really* good. A new K2, Bambu, or even a tuned Ender can produce beautiful terrain with surprisingly little effort. But many terrain systems still assume advanced hobby workflows, slicer tuning, magnets, or complicated setup.

GridSmith actually started on a completely stock Ender 3. No upgrades. No tuning rabbit hole. I wanted to make sure the system worked well on the kind of printer a lot of people already own or start with. Once I felt confident in the results, I upgraded to a K2 and started optimizing around what modern printers are capable of.

I kept coming back to one question:

> What would a terrain system look like if it were designed to be easy first?

That idea became GridSmith.

The core goals are simple:
- fast prints
- beginner friendly
- modular
- organized
- easy to expand
- easy to actually use

That’s why GridSmith uses lightweight reusable base grids and snap-fit tiles instead of magnets or clips. It’s why the tiles are optimized around efficient printing. It’s why the storage trays became such an important part of the system.

I also wanted the system to be approachable from a cost standpoint.

All low-resolution GridSmith tiles are completely free on the major maker platforms:

- [Printables](https://www.printables.com/@Gridsmithio_4636544)
- [MakerWorld](https://makerworld.com/en/@iveld)
- [Thingiverse](https://www.thingiverse.com/Gridsmithio)

The GridSmith tile builder is also free to use.

The only paid portion of the system is the high-resolution STL files, which help support continued development of the project.

I want GridSmith to feel approachable.

Not like a giant hobby commitment. Not like a weekend lost to calibration and assembly. Just something you can print, snap together, and start playing with.

The Tavern Core Set is the first major release, but there’s a lot more coming:
- dungeon tile set
- cave tile set
- outdoor tile set
- expanded storage systems
- map-building tools
- and plenty of iteration along the way

GridSmith is being built openly through real prints, real testing, and real use at the table.

If you’re following along, thanks for being here.

- Levi
